    How to play
    • 6 medium questions, then 14 hard ones, drawn from a bank of checked sequences. Expect fractions, negatives and operation cycles.

    MTRX · Matrices

    Abstract reasoning: the figures follow a pattern across rows, down columns, or step by step. Pick the figure that fills the gap from 8 options.

    Mode
    Example — the answer is filled in
    How to play
    • Grids: look along the rows, down the columns and along the diagonals. Shape, colour, size, number, rotation, position and inner parts can each follow their own rule.
    • Series (numbered 1, 2, 3 …): find what moves, turns, grows or repeats and continue it. Watch out: sometimes only every other figure (1, 3, 5, 7 …) follows the pattern and the ones in between are decoys.
    • Combining: the third figure can take its outer shape from the first and its inner shape from the second.
    • Counting: dots or polygon sides add up, subtract, multiply, or keep a constant total.
    • Overlays: the third figure merges the first two — all marks, only unshared marks, only shared marks, or the first minus the second. Sometimes you have to work backwards.
    • Nine difficulty levels; harder levels stack several rules at once. No two matrices in a row are the same kind.
    • Test: 20 matrices in 10 minutes, level 1 up to 9. Skip and come back if you're stuck.
    • Survival: no end — one level harder every 2 solved. 60 seconds per matrix; 3 misses and you're out.
    • +1 for every correct answer; wrong answers cost nothing, so never leave one blank at the end.

      MCQS · Multiple choice

      Online-assessment style: 20 questions, 5 options each. Wrong answers cost a point, so skip when you're unsure.

      Difficulty
      How to play
      • Tap an option (or press A–E / 1–5). The bottom of the screen flashes green or red.
      • +1 right, −1 wrong, 0 skipped. Skip sends a question to the back of the line.
      • The wrong options are the classic slips: a missed carry, the decimal point one place off, a neighbouring times-table answer. Don't grab the first number that looks about right.
      • Shortcuts: estimate first, check the last digit, count decimal places, and for "which is the largest?" rewrite powers to the same base (47 = 214).

      CNVN · Conviction

      True or false, more or less. Pick your answer, then slide from 50% to 100% to show how sure you are. Read carefully: being sure and wrong costs far more than being unsure.

      Format
      Category
      Difficulty
      Questions

      How scoring works
      • 50% scores 0, right or wrong. Use it when you're guessing.
      • The surer you are, the more you win if right and the more you lose if wrong: at 80% it's +0.84 or −1.56; at 100% it's +1 or −3.
      • Your best play is always to set the slider to what you really believe.
      • Right and wrong are only revealed at the end. Some statements look almost identical, so read every word.
      • Interview: when the 30 seconds run out, your current answer is locked in. No answer stays blank and scores 0. You can't go back.
